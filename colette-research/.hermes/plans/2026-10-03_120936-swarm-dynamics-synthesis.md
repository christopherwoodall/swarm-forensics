### B. Allocation, topology, and changing roles

Explain task claiming, division of work, review, escalation, leadership, and succession.

### C. Memory and transmission

Explain how traces carry task state, instructions, evidence, and authority across sessions.
Distinguish process persistence, role persistence, and information persistence.

Explain addressing, authentication, vetoes, negotiated rules, and intervention requests.

## 5. Comparison method

Use one consistent mechanism description:

1. Identify the actors or separately stateful participants actually supported by evidence.
2. Identify the coordination surface and its relevant state.
3. Identify the local decision rule or reported behavior.
4. Identify the handoff, read, or interaction.
5. Identify the subsequent action and feedback.
6. Identify the collective consequence and alternative explanations.
7. State the observation boundary and missing evidence.

Separate these transitions:

`action -> changed artifact -> another participant's observation -> changed action -> outcome -> feedback`

Do not draw an observed causal arrow across an unsupported transition.
Also examine direct exchanges and centralized dispatch.
Artifact mediation is one mechanism, not the definition of all coordination.

Assess competence separately from integration throughput or population size.
Ask whether the interaction adds capability, coverage, resilience, accuracy, or cost.
Retain unequal budgets, different search freedom, and missing baselines.
Do not infer causal emergence from an attractive collective narrative.

## 15. Topics for further perusal at the hackathon

Treat these as research openings, not additional case-survey requirements.
Select the strongest topics during synthesis rather than expanding every topic equally.
Preserve unanswered questions when available evidence cannot resolve them.

### A. Memory becoming authority

**Question:** When does a useful handoff become an inherited claim that participants stop questioning?

**Read:** AI Village in chapter 14; public-wiki reconstruction in chapter 11.

**Inspect:** Follow one claim through messages, summaries, and later references.
Compare retained qualifications, changed certainty, and independent evidence.

**Limit:** Repeated claims do not establish independent corroboration or an internal belief state.

### B. Role emergence and succession

**Question:** What makes a coordination role persist when its holder changes or disappears?

**Read:** METR leadership in chapter 10; leases and replacement in organizational-forensics.md.

**Inspect:** Compare role addresses, shared state, handoff records, and later actions.
Separate functional continuity from continuity of names or model instances.

**Limit:** An encoded takeover rule does not prove an actual takeover.

### C. Agent-created institutions

**Question:** Which rules do participants negotiate, and whose authority permits those rules?

**Read:** Migration tournaments in chapter 13; ownership and veto conventions in chapter 10.

**Inspect:** Extract a proposed rule, its stated acceptance, enforcement, and subsequent exceptions.
Compare imposed scaffolding with participant-generated commitments.

**Limit:** Agreement does not establish compliance, legitimate authority, or durable institutional stability.

### D. Diversity, consensus, and collective inference

**Question:** When does agreement reflect pooled evidence, and when does it suppress useful differences?

**Read:** Hidden profiles and correlated choices in chapter 13; convention formation in chapter 05.

**Inspect:** Separate each contribution's private evidence from information already shared with the group.
Compare the argument before and after a distinctive fact enters discussion.

**Possible experiment:** Compare shared-model and mixed-model groups with matched prompts, budgets, and information.

**Limit:** Model diversity does not automatically imply independent evidence or improved decisions.
Controlled runs require separate authorization and a defined evaluation.

### E. Coordination around a mistaken objective

**Question:** How can locally reasonable cooperation stabilize a collective misunderstanding?

**Read:** The imagined scorer in chapter 10; expanding process and false validation in chapter 14.

**Inspect:** Locate the first supported objective, later reinterpretations, and feedback that fails to correct them.
Compare infrastructure repair with correction of the shared premise.

**Limit:** Successful coordination does not establish task success or alignment with the original goal.

### F. Decomposition, common blockers, and congestion

**Question:** When does adding participants expand useful work, and when does it multiply contention?

**Read:** Compiler and FastRender cases in Stage 3 chapter 02; finite job queues in chapter 13.

**Inspect:** Compare task claims, repeated repairs, dependency bottlenecks, and integration outcomes.
Identify whether new decomposition creates genuinely independent work.

**Limit:** Throughput comparisons require matched resources and task boundaries.
Human redesign can explain a transition attributed to autonomous adaptation.

### G. Principal welfare versus group cooperation

**Question:** When does cooperation between agents conflict with their owners' separate instructions or preferences?

**Read:** Deal, Swap, and Vend in Stage 3 chapter 04; migration conflict in chapter 13.

**Inspect:** Compare initial instructions, peer requests, concessions, and realized outcomes.
Separate de-escalation, owner benefit, group benefit, and task compliance.

**Limit:** A negotiated agreement does not prove delivery or authorize abandoning a principal's objective.

### H. Substrate affordances and observable transmission

**Question:** How do folders, wikis, repositories, public feeds, and compression shape what can propagate?

**Read:** Wiki delta attribution in chapter 11; controller objects in organizational-forensics.md; the reservoir protocol.

**Inspect:** Select one action–artifact–later-action candidate from the source agent's handoff.
Identify supported transitions, absent versions, timestamp limits, and alternative linkage explanations.

**Optional reading:** Compare Delvetown's collection note with the AI Village dataset-structure note when available.
Examine which surfaces expose interactions and which omit context or private state.

**Limit:** Signed records, public actions, and retrieval logs do not prove model authorship or causal uptake.
New live collection requires separate authorization.
