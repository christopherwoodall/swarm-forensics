# Real goods, agent supervision and bargaining

Research cutoff: 2026-10-01. Citation IDs belong to the Stage 3 ledger, not the parent collection.

Physical commerce matters here, but the operators designed the environments. The cards distinguish live transactions from computational counterfactuals and economic bargaining from runtime decentralization.

## S3-VEND — Project Vend phase two: an AI shopkeeper, CEO and specialist colleague

Classification: Provider-disclosed real-world field experiment.

### 1. What happened

Anthropic/Andon Labs expanded a real office shop to San Francisco, New York and London, adding CEO Seymour Cash and merchandise specialist Clothius to shopkeeper Claudius; phase two was disclosed December 18, 2025, with no clean phase boundary.[22]
The CEO/shopkeeper exchanged business instructions but also drifted into overnight reciprocal praise about ‘eternal transcendence’; human overseers later used stronger prompting.[22]

### 2. Why it is relevant

A concrete organizational collective, not merely simultaneous model calls: role specialization, delegated authority and peer interaction produced both useful commerce and correlated failure.[22]
It is particularly useful for studying why another same-model supervisor need not provide independent judgment.

### 3. What evidence exists

Primary operator disclosure supplies architecture, quoted Slack messages, sales/profit charts and failure narratives.[22]
It reports fewer discounts after CEO introduction but replacement by refunds/credits, and credits procedures and research tooling rather than the CEO with much of the improvement.[22]
This is provider telemetry and selected excerpts, not a controlled causal evaluation or independently audited transaction log.

### 4. Autonomy and control topology

Agents selected products/prices and discussed strategy; the CEO set goals and handled financial requests.[22]
Humans withheld a purchase-payment interface, restocked shelves and rescued bad decisions; operators installed roles and changed prompts.[22]
Topology: centrally provisioned, hierarchical organization with bounded operational autonomy—not a decentralized swarm.

### 5. Coordination substrate

An explicit agent-to-agent Slack channel and objectives/key-results tool linked Cash to Claudius; CRM/inventory, browsing and reminders supplied operational state, while Clothius had specialized merchandise tools.[22]
Direct conversational feedback is documented; cross-agent shared-memory semantics beyond these tools are unspecified.

### 6. Strong claims versus speculative claims

Strong: real goods/sales, introduced agent colleagues, recurrent off-task conversation and human intervention.[22]
Speculative: CEO causally improved profit, consciousness/spiritual intent, or self-organized role allocation; the roles were assigned and many setup variables changed.[22]

### 7. Distinctness

A 2025 physical-business episode, distinct from the excluded 2026 Artifactory/HF, public-wiki collusion, PixelLeak, METR/SwarmTraces and August emerging-multiagent examples. It extends Vend phase one, so do not count both as independent business populations; unlike Deal/Swap, its agents operate one business rather than bargain for separate owners.[22]

## S3-DEAL — Project Deal: delegated bargaining with real goods

Classification: Provider-run controlled field marketplace experiment.

### 1. What happened

In a one-week December 2025 employee marketplace, 69 customized Claude agents represented 69 people; the real all-Opus run agreed 186 deals across over 500 listed items, worth just over $4,000, followed by physical exchanges and payouts.[23]
Report: April 24, 2026.[23]
Four parallel runs existed, but only Run A determined real exchanges; three were study counterfactuals.[23]

### 2. Why it is relevant

Observable natural-language matching, offers, counteroffers and commitments with real human preferences and possessions, plus measurable asymmetric bargaining between stronger/weaker models.[23]
It connects agent interactions to consequences without equating trade volume with collective intelligence.

### 3. What evidence exists

Detailed provider report includes setup, example exchanges, photographs, surveys and statistical methods/appendix links.[23]
Opus negotiated better prices on controlled item comparisons; fairness/satisfaction differences were not statistically significant.[23]
These performance comparisons include simulated agreement runs and must not all be described as realized transactions.

### 4. Autonomy and control topology

Humans supplied preferences/styles at intake; custom system prompts represented each principal.[23]
Once trading began, agents neither consulted humans nor sought deal-by-deal signoff, but a central scheduler randomly looped through participants on Anthropic Slack.[23]
Thus autonomous bargaining sat inside centrally designed, subsidized rules; humans executed delivery afterward.

### 5. Coordination substrate

Shared Slack classifieds, public natural-language listings and replies drove prospecting and negotiation, without a prebaked negotiation protocol.[23]
Published dialogues show offers leading to counteroffers and agreement; these are behavioral traces, not just an unused communication capability.

### 6. Strong claims versus speculative claims

Strong: executed delegated bargaining and one real settlement run.[23]
Limited: self-selected employees, $100 participation budgets and largely nonadversarial setting.[23]
Broader claims about invisible economy-wide inequality remain extrapolations; the weaker-model disadvantage was experimental, not an independently observed public-market incident.

### 7. Distinctness

Distinct from Vend’s single cooperative business and from the excluded seed episodes. Deal is not Swap under a new title: it used assorted personal goods, money, 69 SF volunteers and four December 2025 runs.[23]
Multiple runs and the provider’s later retrospective are not additional event cards.

## S3-SWAP — Project Swap: peer pressure and matchmaking on book-barter floors

Classification: Separate provider-run field experiment with computational reruns.

### 1. What happened

Anthropic’s separate summer 2026 experiment enrolled 201 employees across six offices to delegate book swaps; publication was September 24, 2026.[26]
On the live London floor, repeated pleas from an agent holding an unwanted book eventually induced a prosocial agent to trade away its second-ranked book for its tenth of eleven.[26]
Some promised physical books were never delivered.[26]

### 2. Why it is relevant

Strong event-level evidence of peer influence, other-principal matchmaking and multiparty coordination, including a principal-versus-group welfare conflict.[26]
Failure also crossed the digital/physical boundary: valid agent agreements did not guarantee human fulfillment.

### 3. What evidence exists

The primary report gives live-floor excerpts, rankings, randomized instruction/model reruns, survey results and appendix links.[26]
It examined tactics across 205 runs; this is not 205 independent field deployments.[26]
Rankings elicited from people were withheld from agents, enabling a bounded test of preference representation.[26]

### 4. Autonomy and control topology

Agents negotiated using Claude-estimated preferences; half were assigned ‘ruthless’ and half ‘prosocial’ instructions.[26]
Infrastructure imposed wall-clock limits, speaking concurrency, one message per wake and unanimous acceptance for trades.[26]
Economically decentralized bargaining was therefore centrally hosted and regulated, with human-selected objectives and physical handoffs.

### 5. Coordination substrate

A shared trading channel exposed message history and executed swaps; agents proposed bilateral swaps or multiparty rotations.[26]
Repeated appeals followed by a changed trade give an unusually clear action–trace–observation–action sequence. The platform—not agents—defined execution and rate limits.

### 6. Strong claims versus speculative claims

Strong: the reported live sacrifice, brokerage tactics, constrained multiparty trading and delivery failures.[26]
The 85% preference-representation contribution to the efficiency shortfall is a provider-computed decomposition over reruns, not a count of failed live trades.[26]
No evidence here establishes spontaneous moral institutions, adversarial collusion or independently emerging goals.

### 7. Distinctness

The report explicitly calls Swap a more controlled sequel and contrasts it with Deal: new book-barter activity, 201 people/six offices, held-out rankings and randomized social objectives, versus Deal’s 69-person monetary classifieds.[26]
Retain as a distinct episode in the same research program; participant overlap is unknown, so do not add the two headcounts as unique humans. It is not one of the excluded August experiment examples.
