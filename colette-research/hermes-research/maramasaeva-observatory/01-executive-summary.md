# Executive summary

## What the site is

Murmuration presents itself as an independent observatory for coordinated AI-agent swarms.[1]

It uses public internet exhaust rather than private provider telemetry.[1]

The author separates measurements, background reports, methods, experiments, failures, leads, and guardrails.[1]

The information page states the operational objective clearly.

It seeks earlier, repeatable detection from records that services already publish.[2]

## Bottom line

The site does not claim a newly discovered actor or organization.

Its status section says, “We have not found a new swarm.”[1]

It does claim successful recovery of public-record signatures from known 2026 activity.[1]

The main measurement is a weekly urlquery series over known harness hosts.[1]

The series rises from April, peaks in May and June, then falls near zero.[1]

The page adds submitter aggregates, request shape, and decoded-page structure.[1]

The site does not store report contents, URLs, names, or addresses.[2]

Its strongest new cross-source result is the July K = 2 coincidence.[1]

That result combines ntfy channel bursts with Wayback echo-service captures.[1]

The page labels this corroboration, not proof of a web-wide swarm.[1]

## Evidence hierarchy

The site uses an explicit evidence-grade vocabulary.

E0 means a record written by the service itself.[2]

E1 means an actor's self-description.[2]

E2 means that one event preceded another.[2]

E3 means that a later actor demonstrably read earlier content before acting.[2]

The measurements page currently uses E0 only.[2]

This boundary prevents timing coincidence from becoming causal proof.

The site also distinguishes a weak label from a ground-truth label.[2]

Its Wikimedia bot flag tests ranking ability, not agent identity.[1]

## Strongest reported signals

### DseWiki

A live RecentChanges pull contains 22,802 change entries and 2,819 distinct authors.[1]

The frozen burst rule finds 12 days inside 16–22 June 2026.[1]

The peak occurs on 18 June, with 748 first-seen accounts.[1]

That day also has 832 active accounts and 5,925 edits.[1]

The site reports 48.8% machine-looking names across all 2,819 accounts.[1]

Six ordinary control days fail the burst rule.[1]

The site uses first appearance as a new-account proxy.[1]

It does not reproduce the reported Azure-IP attribution.[1]

### urlquery

The site reports 12,203 reports across listed hosts from 13 April to 21 June.[1]

It reports only 63 reports from 22 June onward.[1]

The known week begins 15 June 2026.[1]

That week contains 1,150 httpbin reports, 506 pie.dev reports, and 851 httpbun reports.[1]

The weekly series uses host-specific baselines.[2]

A high count without a baseline jump is not a finding.[2]

The page reports 135 hashed submitter addresses during the swarm period.[1]

No peak-week submitter appears across the listed hosts.[1]

That structure supports concentration analysis but does not identify an actor.

### Wayback echo-service captures

The pre-registered page-shape rule marks 28 May, 6 July, and 29 September as swarm-like.[1]

The 4 June pie.dev burst is marked tester-like.[1]

The page records 93% swarm-style pages on 6 July.[1]

It records 83% swarm-style pages on 29 September.[1]

A skeptic review weakens the lead because the 2025 baseline is small.[1]

The strongest alternative remains one person or script archiving demo pages.[1]

The archive does not record who triggered a capture.[1]

### ntfy message channels

The later sweep recovers exactly three ntfy burst days.[1]

Those days are 3 March, 6 July, and 8 July 2026.[1]

The 3 March event resembles an archive crawl of ordinary channels.[1]

The 6 and 8 July events show short, one-use, machine-style names.[1]

The 8 July event has 81 names and 81 captures.[1]

All 81 names appear on one archive day.[1]

A larger control yields a narrow confirmation.[1]

The site still warns that a test harness could produce the same shape.[1]

### DseWiki RecentChanges recovery

The site independently recovers the June wiki swarm from one public change log.[1]

It applies the same burst rule to 52 active days.[1]

All 12 burst days fall inside 16–22 June.[1]

The 6–8 July window is null on DseWiki.[1]

That separates the June wiki event from the July message-channel event.[1]

## Strong nulls and corrections

The independent vocabulary detector does not reproduce “cohort,” “please,” or “relay.”[1]

The actor co-editing graph has an adjusted Rand index near zero.[1]

Arrival correlation is only slightly above the raw-activity control.[1]

The Wikimedia model has AUC 0.689 with a 4.6% positive base rate.[1]

Its precision at threshold 0.5 is about 10%.[1]

The behavior-only classifier transfers poorly across wiki platforms.[1]

Its artifact-only control reaches about 0.98 AUC.[1]

The Bluesky candidate is an empty-text hashing artifact.[1]

The HF Spaces candidate resolves into an open ICML-2026 reproduction program.[1]

The npm lead resolves to a 2021 dependency-confusion phenomenon.[1]

The crates.io census finds no qualifying burst.[1]

The certificate-transparency census finds no qualifying burst.[1]

The opaque reasoning-block census finds no cluster.[1]

## Central methodological lesson

The site's own conclusion favors network identity over language or behavior.[1]

Known discoveries used server logs or scanner records with submitter identity.[1]

Text, style, timing, and behavior alone produce weak or confounded results.[1]

The information page states the causal gap directly.

A scanner record shows a request and time, not the program that sent it.[2]

Therefore the site treats baselines, submitter structure, and independent records as necessary context.[2]

## Overall assessment

The pages document a disciplined public-record observatory.

They show strong separation between discovery, corroboration, and attribution.

They preserve nulls and false starts rather than presenting only positive results.[1]

Their strongest evidence concerns known events and public traces.[1]

Their weakest evidence concerns actor identity, hidden migration, and future activity.

The site does not establish a new covert swarm.

It does establish a useful measurement program for public exhaust.
