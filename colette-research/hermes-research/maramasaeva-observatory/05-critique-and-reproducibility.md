# Critique and reproducibility

## What is strong

The site states its detection rule before many measurements.[1]

It records commit hashes for frozen rules.[1]

It compares results with ordinary days, shuffles, or circular shifts.[1]

It keeps failed detectors and corrections in the public log.[1]

It distinguishes known-event reproduction from new discovery.[1]

It avoids public attribution to people, labs, and companies.[1]

It avoids retaining report text and submitter addresses.[2]

It discloses missing sensors and access limits.[1]

The information page gives a clear causal boundary.[2]

A scanner report shows a request and time, not its generating program.[2]

## What the pages establish

The pages establish what their public records contain.

They establish the site's reported counts and rules.

They establish a reproducible presentation of the known urlquery pattern.[1]

They establish two public-record bursts under the site's frozen rules.[1]

They establish several null results and confounded classifiers.[1]

They do not independently establish the external reports listed in the source section.

They do not expose the underlying repository or raw data.

They do not establish who submitted a report.

They do not establish that a capture came from an AI agent.

They do not prove a common operator across dates.

They do not prove migration after the urlquery trace stopped.

## Coverage limits

The urlquery work is search-only and quota-limited.[1]

The echo-service search stopped after HTTP 429 responses.[1]

The Wayback baseline contains only 18 decoded 2025 pages.[1]

Several source feeds are sampled, pruned, or authentication-gated.[1]

The Mastodon census has partial public-timeline coverage.[1]

The Nostr sample does not represent full relay history.[1]

The HF model census does not cover 6–8 July.[1]

The Go-module walk does not cover July.[1]

The Common Crawl fleet test samples one collection.[1]

The live RecentChanges pull is a one-shot measurement.[1]

The site uses first appearance as a new-account proxy.[1]

The site does not reproduce the external Azure-IP attribution.[1]

## Statistical limits

Baseline zero makes any nonzero count appear large.[2]

The site marks baseline-zero rows explicitly.[1]

Small control samples weaken null estimates.[1]

The 2025 Wayback comparison is especially small.[1]

The 8 July confirmation is narrow on the median-lifetime threshold.[1]

The global K = 2 rate is not rare by itself.[1]

The July p-value applies to a held-out window.[1]

It does not establish a universal web-wide event rate.[1]

Several tests use keyword or page-shape heuristics.[1]

The site does not report precision against a labelled, representative field set for those heuristics.[1]

## Source-provenance limits

The measurements page lists primary, secondary, press, and unverified records.[1]

It marks several incident figures as secondary or press.[1]

Its final uncertainty note says some sources were not read at source.[1]

It states that all counts refer to public reports, not distinct actors.[1]

Some visible links are malformed or incomplete.

One Wikipedia URL ends in `Moltboo`.

One Hacker News URL ends after `id=`.

One GitHub URL retains a trailing period.

These links require manual repair before external citation.

Preserve the literal strings in the source inventory.

Do not silently normalize them.

## Reproduction requirements

A full reproduction requires the unpublished repository or equivalent public datasets.

It requires API credentials for urlquery and X.[1]

It requires access to the named public indexes and registries.[1]

It requires matching date windows and rate limits.

It requires the frozen rule commits named by the page.[1]

It requires the same exclusion rules for secrets, names, text, and addresses.[1]

The public pages support conceptual reproduction.

They do not support byte-for-byte computational reproduction.

## Safe interpretation

Use the DseWiki and ntfy results as public-record anomalies.

Use the Wayback results as independently logged page-shape anomalies.

Use K = 2 as cross-record corroboration for selected dates.

Do not use these results for actor attribution.

Do not treat a machine-looking name as proof of an AI.

Do not treat a cloud network as proof of a common operator.

Do not treat absence from one source as absence from the internet.
