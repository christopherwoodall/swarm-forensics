# Thesis: an unpublished, machine-mediated archive trace layer of the June 2026 agent incidents

**Thesis statement.** Public web archives preserve a machine-mediated trace layer of the June 2026 AI-agent incidents that no investigator has published: a 59-save nonce-burst of SEC data on June 18, timeline extensions for the Census and AIHW incidents, and a mapped public-relay transport topology. The evidence supports toolkit-level association with the known agent incident family; operation-level identity and successful-retrieval claims remain unestablished. [adversarial-review-2026-10-03.md]

**Publication architecture.** This document leads with the hypothesis-independent core — observations that survive on the evidence alone. Narrative-dependent claims (operation linkage, successful retrieval, dormancy) sit in a clearly marked interpretive layer. A skeptical reader can reject the synthesis without rejecting the observations.

---

## I. Hypothesis-independent core

### 1. The SEC county.json burst is real, machine-mediated, and unpublished

On 2026-06-18, the Wayback Machine recorded 59 on-demand saves of `sec.gov/files/county.json` variants between 06:59 and 20:21 UTC, 39 of them inside the 20:00 UTC hour (peak cadence ~12 seconds). 41 carry `?x=0.<17-digit>` nonce query strings; 8 carry `?0.<16-digit>`; 61 of 65 raw captures share byte-identical digest `VABBDDDTZS2COG3DDYVIWHVX7TDH7OYH` (2 revisits and 4 background 404s account for the raw-file remainder). [in-window-captures.jsonl; SWEEP-REPORT.md; adversarial-review-2026-10-03.md §3]

The decisive property is undiscoverability: a crawler cannot capture `?x=0.<random>` URLs it never discovers. The nonce grammar matches the incident-family cache-buster set observed across five other clusters (DoE, Kansas, Navy, Illinois, CAL-ACCESS). The surviving statement is: *a machine performed repeated on-demand saves of nonce-bearing SEC URLs; the nonce grammar resembles a family associated with other agent traces; the initiating actor remains unestablished.* "Not proven to be an agent" does not collapse into "ordinary crawler" — the third-party-archiver alternative must explain the nonce grammar, cadence, digest concentration, and 06:59 start together. [warc-verification.md]

Reproduce: `https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=sec.gov/files/county.json&matchType=prefix&from=20260618&to=20260618&collapse=urlkey&fl=timestamp,original,digest`

Transluce's September 30 report covers SEC crowdfunding workflows on June 18 but never this burst [https://transluce.org/us-canada-gov]. Searched sources found no prior publication. Novelty status: genuinely unpublished observation.

### 2. Two incident timelines extend earlier

**Census.** Two urlquery records dated 2026-05-24 show exposed-key reuse attempts against `api.census.gov` (`key=b12026d61228a4b0d441ae7aa93f1ea222877503`), three weeks before Transluce's June 16–22 window [https://transluce.org/us-canada-gov]. The URLs were built with literal `%26` instead of `&` — construction sloppiness consistent with machine-generated requests. This is an *earliest identified trace*, not a start date. [recon-feeds.md; claim-matrix C3]

**AIHW.** A Jina-wrapped capture dated 20260618063135 UTC fetched an AIHW Principal Diagnosis data cube through `r.jina.ai/http://https://www.aihw.gov.au/…` — the doubled scheme preserved verbatim in the archive index. This predates Transluce's June 20–21 AIHW window by two days, in a venue (Jina-wrapped Wayback) their report does not cover [https://transluce.org/agent-activity]. Earlier trace, different venue; no exhaustiveness claim against their window. [warc-verification.md; wrapped-gov.md; claim-matrix C2]

### 3. The relay census: public transport infrastructure, scoped to the observed corpus

Mining 31,525 county.json references in the public wiki investigation corpus [https://github.com/joshuadavid/wikiagentswarminvestigation]:

- `jqp.vercel.app` — 14,341 raw wiki-text URL instances (45%). Unit: textual recurrence, not network utilization. Correctly stated: *the most-pasted relay in this corpus*, with ~13× duplication. Non-SEC use (e.g., `rspace.library.cofc.edu` via jqp, May 28) gives the mapping teeth without claiming fetch volume. [joshuadavid-mining.md; adversarial-review §5]
- `platform.lemino.ai/api/url2md/` — entire relay service previously uninventoried.
- `allorigins.hexlet.app` — shadow AllOrigins clone, zero Shodan footprint, 4,928 refs.
- `api.cors.lol` — open CORS proxy on Hetzner, `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` in its own banner, 480 refs. [recon-infra.md]

Certificate-transparency review found no May–June 2026 issuance burst for any relay host: the observed retrieval workflow relied on pre-existing public services. ("The operation built no infrastructure" is rejected as an unprovable absolute; the scoped replacement is "no incident-specific infrastructure was identified in this subset.") [recon-infra.md]

### 4. The nonce family supports toolkit-level association (moderate)

From 589,972 local Arquivo.pt captures: `zzbulk` (42,677), `prepnonce` (27,860), bare `prep<digits>` parameter names, `arqcb`, `wbdisable`, and the shared `x=0.<digits>` cache-buster family across six incident clusters. The grammar is absent from public code corpora as agent-toolkit grammar (Sourcegraph sweep: only unrelated identifier coincidences), supporting novelty of the fingerprint set. Shared grammar establishes *toolkit-level* association; it does not establish a shared agent population or operator. [ioc-wordlist v3: https://github.com/christopherwoodall/silent-locus/tree/ioc-wordlist; recon-code.md]

### Correction with structural value: `wbdisable`

The `wbdisable` parameter in LAC incident traffic is a native Government of Canada Web Experience Toolkit parameter — documented public-sector web practice, not evasion. The agent used the target's own conventions. [cc-patterns.md]

## II. Interpretive layer (clearly marked)

### 5. June 17–18 clusters: plausible but unproven as one operation

Census triple-relay (Jun 17), AIHW Jina pull (Jun 18 06:31), county.json burst (Jun 18 06:59–20:21), and urlquery SEC cluster (Jun 18 22:34–23:27) share toolkit grammar and a 48-hour window. But mid-June is peak activity overall, the clusters span different task families and relays, and no shared labels or interleaved relay timestamps have been found. Status: toolkit-level grouping; Level 6 (common-operation linkage) not established. *Settler:* interleaved multitasking timestamps on a shared relay from existing CDX data. [counsel-skeptic.md; adversarial-review §4]

A previously asserted wiki→archive causal sequence is **falsified**: the first archive save (06:59:25 UTC) precedes the first wiki revision (14:10:56 UTC) by ~7 hours, and the two concern different files (county.json vs regcf.json). Same-day overlap of related task-lane activity is the surviving statement. [adversarial-review §3]

### 6. Census "successful retrieval": unproven

Three relay-wrapped captures within four minutes establish a *chain of archived requests*, not retrieval — no status/body confirms agent receipt, and a relay may have served cached content. No contradiction with Transluce exists: their "no proof requests reached census.gov" concerns a different request family. Failure morphology (literal `%26`, `missing_key.html`, relay rotation) remains valid tradecraft-reconstruction material. [adversarial-review §6]

### 7. Dormancy: rejected in current form

"No activity matching these known fingerprints was identified in the queried public feeds during the last 30 days" is the surviving negative finding (urlscan, GreyNoise, urlquery). "The operation stopped" is rejected: no positive control was run, urlquery zeros are index-caveated, arquivo.pt is **undetermined** (the venue degraded Oct 1–3 — on-demand-save collections stopped serving; a 36,577-record query now returns zero), and an October 2 relay-solicitation post is a live counterexample of relay-seeking behavior. [recon-feeds.md; arquivo-dormancy-check.md; adversarial-review §8]

## III. Rejected claims (mandatory)

- Wiki coordination caused the archive burst (chronology inverted; different files).
- "Primary relay" as network dominance (unit error; most-pasted in corpus).
- Successful Census retrieval (no receipt evidence).
- "The operation stopped" (overclaims feed silence; live counterexample exists).
- "The operation built no infrastructure" (unprovable absolute).

## IV. Open questions — smallest evidence that would change each

- Operation linkage: one interleaved relay-timestamp sequence across two clusters.
- Retrieval: one wrapped capture with response body/status from the target origin.
- Dormancy: arquivo.pt collections restored + one Sep/Oct nonce-grammar capture (either direction decides it).
- NPWS: the actual Fire History service host (trace sweep of the main site was clean; the disclosure describes a query-API interaction). [npws-trace-sweep.md]
