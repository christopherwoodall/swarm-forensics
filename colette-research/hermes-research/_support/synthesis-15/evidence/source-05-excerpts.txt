## S15-05-P01; upstream lines 129-129

Trap 1: cumulative texts. Attributing the content of a revision to its author attributes the text of every predecessor to that author. In a preliminary pass the same numeric series appeared under five different names, and was counted five times as independent evidence. Mining edit histories for coordination has precedent in Wikipedia research \[ [135](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12748v2#bib.bib135 ""), [59](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12748v2#bib.bib59 "")\], but those studies attribute by diff, and so must we. Every statement about authorship in this paper therefore rests on _deltas_: the text a revision added relative to its predecessor on the same page (12,81912,819 of 14,59114,591 revisions have a non-empty delta; the rest are empty saves of the retry loops discussed in § [5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12748v2#S5.SS1 "5.1 Scale, timing and provenance ‣ 5 Results ‣ The Mechanics of a Swarm:A Reproducible External Reconstruction of an UnintendedAgent-Coordination Episode on a Third-Party Wiki")).

## S15-05-P04; upstream lines 454-454

Requests, delivery and causal use. The export records no reads; the operator’s log records content requests without response bodies or stable agent identifiers (§ [5.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12748v2#S5.SS7 "5.7 Content requests, observed — and what they do not identify ‣ 5 Results ‣ The Mechanics of a Swarm:A Reproducible External Reconstruction of an UnintendedAgent-Coordination Episode on a Third-Party Wiki")). Reconstructed availability is 39.4 % through the page channel and 95.6 % including the last 100 revisions, compared with 12.5 % demonstrable quotation. These are different observations, not nested bounds on total transmission: request missingness, reused names and unobserved channels prevent that ordering. The adoption pattern is compatible with transmission and with shared scaffolding or synchronized starts. The new request-to-adoption association is exploratory; neither its presence nor its attenuation under controls identifies a causal effect. Some controls use later activity, and state matching does not establish the response received, especially for historical-version requests or concurrent saves. Context logs could verify delivery to an agent; identifying causal effects would additionally require justified assumptions or an intervention. The same uncertainty prevents separation of Schelling salience from shared-model action similarity \[ [16](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12748v2#bib.bib16 "")\], behaviour towards an imagined evaluator \[ [14](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12748v2#bib.bib14 "")\], and team reasoning \[ [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12748v2#bib.bib13 "")\].
